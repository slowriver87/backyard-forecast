How the score works

Each day gets a score from 0 to 100. Only daylight hours from 7 AM until 30 minutes before sunset count, since that is when he is out in the yard.

  • Rain (40%): 100 minus the highest chance of rain during those hours.
  • Heat (35%): based on the hottest daylight "feels like" temperature. 65°F or cooler scores 100. 95°F or hotter scores 0.
  • Humidity (25%): compares the day's dew point to normal for that date. Normal scores 50. 8°F drier scores 100. 8°F muggier scores 0.

Great is 75 and up. Good is 60 to 74. Fair is 45 to 59. Rough is below 45. Up to three days scoring 60 or more get a "Best pick" tag.

What the numbers mean

Dew point is the best single measure of how sticky the air feels. Higher means muggier. Around 60°F feels fine to most people. 70°F and up feels heavy.

Feels like is the National Weather Service heat index. It combines temperature and humidity. Below about 80°F it equals the air temperature.

Nice hours counts the daylight hours that are 80°F or cooler (feels like) with under a 30% chance of rain. More nice hours means a longer stay outside.

Afternoon check looks only at 1 PM until he comes in. It compares the hottest the afternoon will feel with a typical July afternoon at the same spot, from 20 years of history. Labels: 80°F or cooler is an easy afternoon. 81 to 90°F means keep an eye on him. Above 90°F means short visits only.

Best stretch of the day is the 4-hour block with the best mix of low rain chance and cooler feels-like temperature.

How much to trust it

Days 1 to 7 come from the National Weather Service, the official US forecast. It does not go past 7 days, so days 8 to 10 come from Open-Meteo, which serves the NOAA and European computer models.

Forecasts get less reliable the further out they go. The confidence label (High for days 1 to 3, Medium for 4 to 7, Low for 8 to 10) is a general rule, not a measured accuracy number.

The score rules are a rule of thumb picked for this dog's stated preferences. They are not a veterinary standard. Very hot days call for your own judgment no matter the score.
